1. How this works

CAC on Stellar is issued by a dedicated issuer account and held/traded through a separate distributor account. Every unit of CAC that exists on Stellar is only backed because a matching amount of real CAC sits, untouched, in a dedicated wallet on the actual CodexaCoin chain. The claim being made is simple: reserve balance ≥ issued supply, always.

3. Early trade history was project-seeded, not organic demand

On 2026-08-03, the project placed one small round-trip trade on the CAC/XLM order book so third-party chart viewers (like stellar.expert) wouldn't show a completely empty chart for a brand-new listing: a project-controlled account bought ~28 CAC for ~2 XLM (tx), then sold it back for ~1.87 XLM (tx). Both sides of both trades were project-controlled accounts — this is not evidence of real buy/sell demand, and you should not treat the resulting one buy + one sell candle as a signal that anyone else is trading CAC. We're disclosing it here specifically so nobody mistakes it for that.

4. What this is not

This is not a formal, independently-audited attestation. Nobody outside the CodexaCoin project has reviewed this process. It's a self-reported, but independently checkable, comparison of two public on-chain numbers — the same minimum bar any issuer of a reserve-backed asset should clear, not a substitute for real third-party audit. The issuer's Stellar signing key has not yet been locked (multisig or master-weight-zero); until it is, the project retains the technical ability to issue more CAC on Stellar than is backed. We haven't done so, and have no plan to, but you should not take that on faith either — that's exactly why this page checks the numbers live rather than describing intentions.
